# v0.2 summary

Status on October 20, 2026: v0.2 is feature-complete on branch `v0.2` (19 commits ahead of `main`, latest `8e93b10`). It is not pushed or merged, and its release gate is not yet met: battle tuning, a human playtest and a few smaller items remain. They are listed in [next-steps.md](next-steps.md).

## What v0.2 is

v0.2 turns the v0.1 remake into a deeper strategy game. Battles reward terrain, tactics and timing. The campaign has plunder, prisoners, unrest and diplomacy with consequences. A second scenario, difficulty presets and a Hegemon victory add replay value. Rules stay deterministic and fair: queries never draw randomness, a rejected command changes nothing, and no difficulty gives rivals free resources.

The ruleset is `baye-atlas-2`, and saves are version 2. Version 1 saves still load: they are migrated without drawing any randomness. The full rules are in [v0.2-rules.md](v0.2-rules.md).

## What changed

**Battles**

- Counterattacks, and a forecast of casualties for both sides before you attack.
- Tactic points, and three new tactics: raid supplies, ambush and rockfall.
- Zone of control: entering a tile next to an enemy ends a move. Cavalry can slip through one such tile.
- Morale states: wavering units hit softer, and routed units cannot attack. An army whose every unit is routed breaks.
- Flanking bonuses, and officer capture by a melee killing blow or encirclement.
- Supply days chosen when marching, a weather forecast, and walls and gates for well-defended cities.
- Four battlefield profiles (river, mountain, forest, plains), and centre-out deployment.
- A unit cannot stop on its own camp, and a routed unit cannot seize the enemy's.
- A second tactical AI (v2) that scores every move. The v1 AI is kept, and v2 beats it in mirrored battles.

**Campaign**

- Plunder your own city, raid an enemy city, or sack one you conquer, each with an exact preview. A garrison weaker than the raiders stays behind its walls.
- Prisoners can be persuaded, released or executed. AI houses decide about their prisoners too.
- Officer levels and traits (benevolent, fierce, cunning, loyal, greedy).
- Unrest and revolts, and departures in January and July.
- Alliance odds, breaking alliances (at a reputation cost), and coalitions against a runaway leader.
- Rival personalities, AI joint attacks and convoys.
- Troop reorganization.
- Historical events that fire only when their conditions hold, and nine everyday events.

**Ways to play**

- Two scenarios: _A divided realm_ (190 AD) and _The eve of Guandu_ (200 AD, with Ma Chao added in Chang'an).
- Story, Standard and Warlord difficulty. Challenging houses get a two-month opening truce on Standard.
- A Hegemon victory: hold Luoyang and 16 cities for 12 months.
- Optional aging, and fog of war on rival cities.
- An adviser with first-campaign goals, which warns when a rival nears the Hegemon title.

**Interface:** the battle panel, a march planner, a post-battle recap, a threat overlay, a prisoner panel, the adviser and monthly-report entries, and level and morale badges. Every new string is in English and Chinese. The map and battle panel load on demand.

**Tooling**

- A player bot and `npm run diagnostics:ai -- --bot`, which plays 36 campaigns and reports every balance target.
- Balance levers that can be swept without code edits (`DIAGNOSTIC_TUNING`), and a defensive "turtle" bot mode.
- An auto-resolve benchmark (`npm run test:perf`) and a frame-rate harness (`npm run test:fps`).

## How it was built

1. **Plan.** [The gameplay improvement plan](archive/gameplay-improvement-plan.md) set the tasks, the balance targets (§6) and ten designer decisions (§7).
2. **Phases 1–3.** These implemented the plan: rules, campaign AI, scenarios and the interface.
3. **Review.** [The v0.2 review](archive/v0.2-review.md) audited the result. Fixes followed for the bot, the docs, routed units, the truce and rally range.
4. **Tracks T1–T5.** These finished the review items, after a second round of decisions (D1–D7).
5. **Wave A.** This added measurement and tooling for the remaining work: battle diagnostics, tuning knobs, the turtle check, the frame-rate harness, and the scenario accessors.

## Quality evidence

On `8e93b10`:

| Check                                                 | Result                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `npm test`                                            | 332 passed, 1 skipped (the opt-in legacy-hash regeneration)                                    |
| `npm run test:e2e`                                    | 51 passed                                                                                      |
| `npm run test:production`                             | 6 passed                                                                                       |
| `npm run test:perf`                                   | 10-versus-10 auto-resolve, 95th percentile about 22 ms (budget 50 ms)                          |
| `npm run test:fps`                                    | 60 fps at normal speed and with the CPU slowed 4× and 6×; about 10 fps under software graphics |
| `npm run build`, `format:check`, `validate:portraits` | pass                                                                                           |

Balance, from the 36-campaign bot run (96 months, both scenarios, Standard). [v0.2-notes.md](v0.2-notes.md) has the full table.

- **Met:**
  - survival of Approachable and Balanced houses;
  - day-limit endings, and camp captures by day 3;
  - upsets in contested battles, and supply run-outs;
  - supply choices, plunder use, and raids that fight (65%);
  - coalitions, captures and AI prisoner bounds;
  - ambush and rockfall use;
  - auto-resolve speed, and v2 beating v1.
- **Missed:**
  - median battle length (4 days; the target is 5–12);
  - close battles (3.8%; the target is ≥ 30%);
  - move-only actions (61.2%; the limit is 60%);
  - unification (0 of 36 runs).

None of this replaces a human playtest, which has not been done yet.
