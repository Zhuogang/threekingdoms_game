- Resolved battle duration and active battle age are separate measurements.
- Developer traces retain at most the requested 0–10,000 events (default 200) and keep cumulative counts after retention fills. Traces are scoped to synchronous simulation calls, disabled in normal play, excluded from saves and hidden from player reports.
- Campaign traces distinguish rejected/eligible/selected candidates, committed dispatches, execution rejection and no eligible campaign. Governor and tactical traces record actual execution. Detailed candidate values will expand with the corresponding policy tickets; existing observations do not claim to explain alternatives the old policy never evaluated.

## V04-02: food forecasts that follow the bill

Battle charging and AI supply evaluation now use one pure fixed-point ration calculation: healthy and wounded personnel, double cavalry weight, and the saved unpaid fraction. A 1,200-person infantry force consumes 48 food/day; 500 food covers about 10.42 days. The campaign passes its existing fraction to tactical evaluation without duplicating saved state.

The next economic deadline comes from absolute day and the saved settlement cursor. August work forecasts August's bill; September retains its larger harvest; a pending saved bill remains due immediately. After all city bills are paid, monthly autonomy forecasts the following boundary. The old optional month argument remains accepted for callers but cannot alter the forecast.

A pure city forecast separates stock, legal availability, reservations, accrued debt, remaining service, production and projected balances. Current paid-personnel credits reduce today's service once. Known friendly arrivals use the actual route ticks and march rations, destination ownership/commitment, warehouse capacity and delivery deadline. Arriving troops incur city service only after arrival; a late convoy cannot fund the current bill. Forecasts assume no new orders, battles or random monthly events. They can evaluate the state returned by an existing city action; previewing never applies the action. Actual monthly billing rates and accounting order are unchanged.

## V04-03: one activation or one qualifying hit

Confuse now targets a living enemy even after that enemy acted. The status persists across rollover and is consumed once when the unit next skips its scheduled move/action opportunity, including a routed unit's hold. Rally can clear it first; repeated Confuse does not stack skipped activations. Dead formations do not acquire phantom turns.

Inspired persists until one ordinary attack, counterattack or Ambush hit consumes the bonus. Fire/Rockfall neither benefit nor consume it, and repeated Rally does not stack multipliers. Forecasts retain the same damage calculation as execution. The existing status enum already serializes these pending effects, so no save field or format migration was necessary. Previews, status labels, English/Chinese logs and Wiki explain the clarified lifetime.

## V04-05: bounded maneuver and formation coordination

Each unspent formation compares at most six legal destinations, including its current tile. The shortlist preserves a useful shot, a safe position and route progress; full ordinary/tactic/hold comparisons then account for the next exchange. Actual movement still uses the existing terrain, water, ZOC and occupied-cell rules. After every action, the remaining formations are reconsidered; a cheap finisher can leave the stronger formation free, and a kill can open a passage for the next formation. Ranged attacks retain the existing range-only legality.

Enemy threats include reachable firing positions and return hits. Forecasting omits the moving unit's old occupancy/ZOC so a vacated cell cannot provide imaginary protection. Its destination can constrain the enemy further; this is a conservative one-response estimate, not a simulation of all future formation orders. Long route fields allow friendly formations to move later, while every immediate move enforces all current blockers. Geometry-keyed threat fields, per-board capability values and unchanged exchange forecasts avoid duplicate work; a replay fixture compares cached choices with fresh evaluations and commits them through player commands.

Scores remain heuristic casualty-equivalent preferences: a full approach move earns approximately 90% of a forecast normal hit, with a 40-per-cell floor; entering contact is weighed against the next exchange, including return fire. Ordinary formations discount next-exchange exposure to 65%; routed formations use the full estimate and prefer survival or legal Rally access. Blocking an ally's sole exit costs 80 (15 for two exits). A 0.1-per-movement-cost tie-break favors staying when positions are otherwise equivalent. The last day has no approach reward; the final three days halve that reward when the current timeout policy already favors the side, and add 15% immediate hit/finish value when behind. Formation ordering adds half the best action's advantage over its next different target, capped at 100. These weights need the paired calibration in V04-09 and do not predict victory probabilities.

Withdrawal still uses the existing opening-force/enemy-force thresholds and campaign-provided return-route permission. At that threshold, developer traces compare reachable next-hit exposure with an advisory withdrawal cost using the existing orderly capture risk and non-returning militia; this collects evidence without changing retreat or capture rules. No extra formation actions, RNG draws, saved fields or battle-ending rules were introduced.

Validation: **189 targeted cases passed, including the two unresolved strategic expected failures**, across battle rules, statuses, campaign/save continuation, experience, inspection, feedback and evidence. Ten new movement/coordination cases cover both seats, safe move-and-shot, routed wounded troops, cheap finishing hits, a kill opening a narrow passage, fresh/cache decision parity, legal command replay, vacated-cell threats, Rally access, final-day holding and permitted/blocked withdrawal. Existing water-crossing, detour and fixed-seed non-stalling cases passed. TypeScript passed. The former fixed-position Fire fixture now gives the already-acted enemy no future activation; the campaign status fixture gives the caster no remaining move and ample supplies, so escape or a useful supply raid cannot replace the cast whose lifetime it tests.

All four isolated performance gates passed their unchanged **50 ms p95** limit, with 30 measured days and three warm-up days per fixture. Opening-turn p95 was 24.6 ms (24×20) and 21.5 ms (48×32); mixed 10v10 engaged and congested 48×32 p95 was 46.6 ms and 47.6 ms respectively. Measurements used Node 22.23.2 on the container's ARM Neoverse-V2, Linux 6.8.0-1046-nvidia-64k, with no concurrent agent test/simulation jobs. ## V04-06: affordable forces and credible exposure

The planner shortlists three legal adjacent source/target pairs and evaluates at most three deduplicated whole-officer parties for each: small, mixed and maximum safe. Each party receives its own actual route and supply estimate. The ten-officer limit, legal garrison minimum, protected order stock and one successful monthly dispatch per faction remain unchanged. This bounded shortlist can miss a worthwhile fourth pair, and its multiple-plan option is greedy rather than a global assignment solver.

Battle opening and strategic assessment now share the actual formation grouping and militia roster. The estimate uses real attack/counterattack forecasts, troop type, range, force, intellect, experience and available tactics, with a fixed map-template mobility/defense allowance. It never reads a future seed or rolls out combat during planning. A power ratio below 0.8 rejects routine conquest; positive viable opportunities beat hold. Cost ranking discounts expected losses, provisions, committed healthy soldiers, travel and exposure. Losses remain a coarse ranking estimate, not a probability or promised outcome.

Incoming threats include only currently hostile campaign journeys with living, owned, healthy officers actually in that party. ETA follows real route ticks rather than a stale display date; allied, returning and noncombat traffic does not count. Before dispatch, a pure departure view leaves wounded personnel at home and forecasts the next unpaid city bill. Negative available food or credible invading power above 90% of the remaining defense blocks departure. The horizon is the expedition's round trip plus 30 days, capped at 90. Multiple incoming armies are conservatively combined; future desertion, battle losses and treaty expiry are not predicted. Commit rechecks current force, home debt and invasion alongside existing legality before allocating IDs or debiting stock.

Waiting traces record the failed party's force/food deficit or defensive blocker. Replanning can act after the condition changes; persistent preparation belongs to V04-08. Actual officer IDs, healthy troops, cavalry, detached wounded, source stock and rations agree between preview and committed journey.

Calibration used six compositions across plain, hill and river routes, both control seats, development seeds 17/31 and untouched held-out seeds 101/137: **72 resolved battles per cohort**, with actual campaign ration charging and 30-day starting provisions. Development exposed a bad cavalry-versus-archer admission; accounting for range, return hits and clipped overkill corrected it before held-out evaluation. ## V04-07: work that meets the deadline

Governors compare buying food with farming using each action's actual next-bill projection. If both cover the protected reserve, recurring farming wins; otherwise the action reducing the shortfall most wins. An approaching hostile army also requires available stock, rather than depending on a future harvest. Shared city effects feed the forecast, including recruitment's new mouths, reduced population, and relief's actual population/loyalty effects. Relief execution now uses that same helper; costs and effects have not changed. Spending cannot leave projected wages or protected orders unfunded, and optional spending cannot create a food deficit. A partial food remedy remains useful when one work point cannot cover the entire shortage.

Outside urgent work, farming and commerce are compared by their actual production increments over the next three settlements, including caps, rounding and September. The existing market exchange of ten food per gold supplies an ordinal comparison. This is a bounded investment preference, not an optimal economic policy; scarce-food buffers and current obligations take priority. Training, talent quotas, same-type reserves and existing action authority retain their established checks.

The shared threat summary replaces the blanket foreign-journey freeze. Allied/noncombat traffic and empty manifests do not block domestic work. Before a real invasion, governors can buy food, restore city/officer loyalty, recruit within projected obligations, and assign existing ready reserves. They organize available reserves before optional activity, retaining approved troop reservations but releasing the ordinary 1,000-soldier unassigned buffer for defense. Moving those existing soldiers adds no mouths, free work points or new troops. Active battles remain a hard restriction; reorganizing officers, pending appointments and reserved commanders remain unavailable. Unsupported food purchases may leave a constrained wait, while free reserve organization and urgent loyalty work can still help. New threats invalidate stale plans.

## V04-08: bounded faction preparation through real orders

Each living NPC faction now keeps one advisory objective: defend, recover, prepare conquest, or conquer. Normal ten-day work boundaries review it before and after city work, with another review before monthly dispatch. A useful source/target pair receives a place in the existing three-pair shortlist and remains preferred while its value is at least 80% of the best comparable opportunity. This modest retention preference is a policy parameter, not a calibrated probability. An actual invasion takes priority; another safe city can still use the existing monthly campaign allowance without replacing the primary defense objective.

Preparation records a commander, food/force targets, current deficits, start/review days and at most two existing order IDs. These fields reserve no resources. Governors acquire actual food, recruit through the same population/gold/work costs, and organize existing same-type reserves toward the force target. They retain all readiness, appointment, order-reservation and talent limits. Food targets use the current departure/home-bill forecast and are revised as actual work and deliveries change readiness; they do not promise future income or a particular departure date. The planner screens force deficits against available population, command capacity and an optimistic 18-recruitment-action ceiling over three months. Actual action budgets still govern every step.

One suitable rear city can dispatch provisions and gold through a real requisition journey. Donor selection protects its next bill, a two-month food buffer (minimum 500), 80 gold, and any real incoming threat or active battle. The recipient receives nothing before legal arrival. Candidates must arrive before an observed defense/food deadline or the preparation's 90-day horizon. There is at most one active support operation per faction and one support departure per 30 days; redirected cargo still occupies that operation until its real return party lands. The first policy deliberately moves resources only; officers and armies prepare locally. The shared NPC order path also supports actual troop contingents, but automatic officer redistribution is deferred rather than adding a second planner.

Internal NPC previews and commits validate the acting faction against both cities, real owned/ready officers, existing troop reservations and cargo. They share route, party, supply, debit and arrival code with player shipments, without changing player identity. Public commands ignore actor overrides, and player answers cannot access NPC orders. An unready NPC request allocates no order, reservation or ID. Journey factories cannot inject officers or cargo; healthy troops and cavalry must match the actual party, with wounded left at their source. A lost receiver uses the existing refuge/return journey with its cargo intact.

A still-unready preparation expires after 90 days and enters a 30-day recovery review. Ownership, alliance, route and commander changes cancel stale preparations explicitly; successful conquest becomes completed recovery. Current world references and order associations are reconciled after commands, arrivals, succession and load. Detailed rival objectives remain internal and in bounded developer traces. The optional, bounded `factionObjectives` extension retains the v3 save envelope: older compatible saves acquire no resources or progress and initialize memory at a normal planning boundary. Duplicate/malformed objectives are rejected; stale references are safely reconciled, while missing/foreign order associations are removed.

### Cityless returns: diagnosis and approved correction

The first long-run investigations expose a distinct limitation: a faction can have no cities, no active battle and only return parties waiting for a friendly refuge, yet remain alive indefinitely. Exact 190 Cao held-out (`970301`) and Yuan development (`610304`) replays reach day 2,880 in that state. Cao's parties were due on days 127/138; Yuan's on day 229. They still hold their free officers, wounded/healthy personnel and zero carried food. These are not viable surviving campaigns. Valid monthly/final saves remain in `.artifacts/v04/{cao,yuan}-policy-96m-observed/`; the portable [return-party extract](evidence/cityless-return-parties.json) preserves the final parties, officer locations, configuration and save hashes.

In the pre-correction runtime `178902b`, the refuge selector, redirect helper and arrival handler were byte-identical to `10c6866`, as were the calendar and prisoner/succession modules. With no reachable friendly city, arrival handling retained the waiting party; the existing house rule eliminates a faction only when it has no free officer. This was inherited behavior, though changed opponents altered how often an opening reached it. The diagnostic's `playerEliminated: false` must therefore be read alongside city ownership and actual military activity. The matrices above preserve those earlier rules and are historical comparisons, not validation of the correction below.

The user explicitly approved **limited surrender** on October 29. A settled return party at a hostile, non-allied city now surrenders only when its faction has no cities, active campaigns or active battles. Friendly, allied and independent destinations remain exempt; any active battle at the destination defers settlement. A campaign arriving that same day still counts as a recovery prospect before its battle opens. An actual recovery also establishes the recovered city as capital when the former capital is lost, correcting an invalid-save case exposed by the recovery test.

Officers retain their house, healthy and wounded contingents, troop type and experience in captivity. Escorted captives change custody; the receiving house's own captive is liberated. Remaining gold/food transfers once, and warehouse overflow remains in a real waiting cargo journey owned by the receiving house. No new save field, random roll or elimination rule is introduced. Existing succession and elimination run after settlement. Both languages explain the rule in the report and Wiki.

The [surrender evidence](evidence/stranded-return-surrender.json) pairs the same real saved inputs against old and new code. The two 190 cases above and the 200 Cao economy-first case all remain cityless and alive after one day on the old runtime; all three correctly end on **day 2,881** on the new runtime. All ten stranded officers retain their healthy/wounded counts, experience and type; RNG is unchanged and every output reloads identically. This establishes old-save continuation, not fresh campaign balance.

The same extract preserves two additional fixed-policy long probes on the **pre-surrender** runtime. Passive 190 Liu survives twelve months but is eliminated on **day 867** after Cao captures Pingyuan. Economy-first 200 Cao reaches day 2,880 with no cities or active battles and seven officers stranded in one waiting return at Jianye, so its apparent survival also depended on the old rule. These outcomes limit the earlier twelve-month survival claim; neither scripted probe establishes human fairness.

Validation for the correction: **21 focused cases**, **998 broader unit/integration cases**, TypeScript and the production build pass. Focused coverage includes both player seats, same-day recovery, third-party destination battles, monthly settlement, cargo caps, captive liberation, repeated settlement and full save/reload equality. Four new production flows cover both languages and both player seats, visible explanations, acknowledgement persistence and identical continuation from the original save. Raw outputs: `.artifacts/v04/stranded-return-{focused-final,build-final,production-final}.log`, `stranded-return-unit-tests-final.json`, `stranded-save-comparison-final.json`. Fresh campaign comparisons remain pending for this rule change.
