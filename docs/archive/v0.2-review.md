# v0.2 implementation review: corrections and critical improvements

> **Archived:** the v0.2 review and work log. Open work and decisions now live in [next-steps.md](../next-steps.md); [v0.2-summary.md](../v0.2-summary.md) summarizes the result. This file is not updated. See the [documentation index](../README.md).

Review date: October 20, 2026. Branch `v0.2` at commit `35b51dd`. Scope: the whole v0.2 implementation measured against [the gameplay improvement plan](gameplay-improvement-plan.md). The review covers the engine, campaign rules, AI, saves, scenarios, UI, docs, tests, and diagnostics tooling.

This document is a work list for later implementation sessions. Every item gives the location, the evidence, a suggested fix, and a test that would prove the fix. Items marked **Fixed** have since been changed; the rest are still open.

## How the review was done

1. Read every v0.2 rule module line by line against its plan task card: `battle.ts`, `campaign.ts`, `world.ts`, `plunder.ts`, `officers.ts`, `supply.ts`, `aiPolicy.ts`, `events.ts`, `advisor.ts`, `fog.ts`, `migrate.ts`, `platform/saves.ts`, `content/guandu.ts`, and the relevant UI.
2. Checked the test suite against each acceptance criterion.
3. Ran throwaway probe tests (deleted afterwards) to confirm suspected behavior on the current code:
   - single-rule battle setups;
   - 30 auto-resolved 10-versus-10 battles;
   - passive-player campaigns: 4 in the 190 scenario and 2 in the Guandu scenario, 96 months each;
   - player-bot campaigns: 36 runs (2 scenarios × 6 houses × 3 seeds), 48 months each;
   - a "turtle" player that never attacks: 15 runs, 36 months each.
4. Re-read the last saved bot diagnostics (`.artifacts/ai-diagnostics/bot.json`, 12 campaigns, 48 months each).

Severity levels:

- **High:** fix before relying on the results, or before calling v0.2 balanced.
- **Medium:** a real rules or gameplay defect, or a design gap a player will notice.
- **Low:** polish, consistency, or tech debt.

## High

### H1. The player bot is reckless, so bot-based metrics are unreliable

**Where:** [tools/playerBot.ts](../../tools/playerBot.ts), lines 94–110, 117–165 and 185–195.

**Evidence:**

- The bot musters every reserve soldier but 300, then marches **every** available officer as soon as `power > 1.2 × target`. This leaves the home city with at most 300 militia.
- It plunders its own cities whenever a neighbor is 2.8× stronger: 81 own-city plunders in 12 campaigns.
- It sacks every rich target, which halves support and invites revolts.
- Results:
  - Last saved diagnostics: the bot was eliminated in 11 of 12 campaigns within 48 months. `unifiedRuns` is 0.
  - Probe on current code: the bot was eliminated in 25 of 36 runs. In the 190 scenario Liu Bei died by month 4–7 in every seed; in Guandu, Liu Bei died at month 2 and Ma Teng at months 4–6.
  - A turtle player that never attacks survived all 15 runs for 36 months. So the game is survivable, and the bot is the problem.
- Every bot-derived §6 metric is skewed by this, including months to unify, captures, plunder share, supply choice and coalition rate.

**Fix:**

1. Keep a garrison. Leave at least one officer, and at least 40% of the city's strength, behind (the same rule as `AI_JOINT_COMMIT`).
2. Never march out of a city that borders a stronger enemy.
3. Plunder only when the city would otherwise fall this month, never more than once per city per year.
4. Sack only when support will stay above 30 afterward.
5. Add a `--scenario` option to the diagnostics so Guandu is covered.
6. Report survival rate, median turn of elimination and `unifiedRuns` next to `monthsToUnifyMedian`. A median over winners alone hides survivorship.

**Done when:** the bot survives at least 80% of 48-month runs for the houses rated "Approachable" and "Balanced". Then re-run every §6 metric and rewrite the table in `v0.2-notes.md`.

**Status: fixed (committed in `d1dc1cf`).** All six fix items are implemented:

- [tools/playerBot.ts](../../tools/playerBot.ts) keeps a garrison and holds cities that border a stronger enemy. It also refuses to march when a neighbouring rival could take the garrison left behind, alone or in a joint attack. Own-city plunder is limited to cities that would otherwise fall, once per city per year, and sacks require support of 30 or more afterwards. It recruits first in threatened border cities.
- `tools/ai-diagnostics.mjs --bot --scenario=divided-realm-190,guandu-200` covers Guandu.
- The output reports survival at 48 months and at the full horizon, the median elimination turn, runs lost to a rival, and `unifiedRuns` beside `monthsToUnifyMedian`. Each is given overall, per rating and per house.

Results on 3 seeds × 96 months × both scenarios (36 runs):

- **Approachable and Balanced houses:** survived 24 of 24 runs at 48 months.
- **Challenging houses:** survived 10 of 12.
- **All houses at 96 months:** survived 34 of 36.

Unification is still 0 of 36 because the bot plays defensively, and [v0.2-notes.md](../v0.2-notes.md) records this as a missed target.

### H3. Rules documentation errors

**Where and what:**

- [docs/v0.2-rules.md](../v0.2-rules.md):
  - Line 27 says "raids **and sacks** use `RAID_DAY_LIMIT`". Only raids do; sacks use 30 days (`campaign.ts:2397`).
  - Line 74 says "Yan is bolder". `aggressionOffset: +0.3` raises the attack threshold, so Yan is **more defensive**.
  - Line 74 says "Cao is wary of low supply". `targetPreference: 'lowSupport'` means Cao **prefers targets with low public support**.
  - The capture row says "may be captured". Capture is deterministic: a melee killing blow or encirclement always captures.
  - The morale row says "every unit recovers 3 per day". Only units that were fed and not hit that day recover.
  - The aging row says "over 60". The code includes age 60 (`OLD_AGE = 60`, death chance `(age − 59) × 3%`, capped at 60%).
- [docs/implementation.md](../implementation.md): only a v0.2 banner was added. The working agreement (§0.2) requires the rule tables themselves to be updated: persuasion, scouting/fog, the no-counterattack and no-natural-death statements, the march food formula, the battle food formula, and the 24-city ending.

**Fix:** correct the lines above, then rewrite the affected `implementation.md` tables. Do the latter once v0.2 rules are frozen.

**Fixed:** every line above is corrected in `v0.2-rules.md`. `implementation.md` now describes v0.2 (persuasion, fog and scouting, counterattacks, optional natural death, march and battle food at `ceil(troops / 25)`, the Hegemon ending, the opening truce, and the v0.2 campaign AI), and the "describes v0.1" banner is gone.

## Medium

### M1. Routed units still counterattack

**Where:** `canCounter` in [src/game/battle.ts:751](../../src/game/battle.ts). It checks troops, confusion and range, but not morale.

**Evidence:** probe. A defender at morale 10 (routed) answered a melee hit with "counterattacks: 249 casualties". This contradicts the P2-3 rule "Routed: can only move or hold". The attack forecast shows the same counter.

**Fix:** add `!isRouted(target)` to `canCounter`. Decide whether wavering units counter at full rate; they currently counter with the ×0.85 wavering penalty.

**Test:** a routed target never counters, and its forecast shows `counterMin = counterMax = 0`. Regenerate the legacy hashes.

**Fixed:** `canCounter` now requires `!isRouted(target)`, so forecasts show no counter either. Wavering units still counter, with the ×0.85 penalty. This change pushed the mirrored 6-versus-6 test below 16/20 decisive battles. The cause was an existing AI stall: starving survivors rallied each other every day until the day limit. So `planUnitV2` no longer scores Rally while its side has no supply days left; the unit approaches or attacks instead. Test: `battle-rules.test.ts` › counterattacks › "does not answer from a routed defender". Legacy hashes regenerated.

### M2. A routed player unit can seize the enemy camp

**Where:**

- `checkOutcome` in [src/game/battle.ts:402–409](../../src/game/battle.ts) accepts any living unit.
- `getReachableTiles` lets routed units move (correct by spec).
- The AI already refuses this (`planUnitV2` checks `!routed`), so the two sides play by different rules.

**Evidence:** probe. A routed attacker moved onto the defender's camp and the battle ended with outcome `attacker`.

**Fix:** a routed unit cannot capture a camp. Either skip routed units in `checkOutcome`, or remove the enemy camp from a routed unit's reachable tiles. The second is clearer in the UI.

**Test:** a routed unit next to the camp cannot win; a steady unit still can.

**Fixed:** both. `getReachableTiles` drops the enemy camp for a routed unit, so the UI never offers it and `battleMove` rejects it. `checkOutcome` also ignores routed units as a safeguard. Test: `battle-rules.test.ts` › "a routed unit next to the enemy camp cannot seize it; a steady unit can".

### M3. A unit on its own camp makes camp capture impossible

**Where:** movement treats every occupied tile as blocked (`paths`), and camp capture requires standing on the camp. The AI camp guard is allowed to stand on its own camp (`distance ≤ 1`).

**Evidence:** probe confirms the attacker can never reach an occupied enemy camp. The plan lists this as problem #3 ("A unit parked on its own camp tile blocks that camp entirely"), but no task addressed it.

**Fix (designer decision, because D4 keeps instant capture):** choose one.

- (a) Units cannot end a move on their own camp.
- (b) Killing or routing the occupant of a camp counts as capturing it.
- (c) Accept and document it as a defensive option.

**Test:** whichever rule is chosen, plus an AI guard test.

**Decision (D1):** (a). A unit cannot end a move on its own camp, but may pass through it. Planned in T1.3.

### M4. Rally silently cures confusion

**Where:** `castSkill` in [src/game/battle.ts:1044–1047](../../src/game/battle.ts). It sets `status = 'inspired'` over `'confused'`.

**Evidence:** probe. A confused ally had 0 attack targets. After an ally's rally it had 1 and could act the same day. The AI values this at +250 (`planUnitV2`). Neither the plan nor the docs mention it.

**Fix:** decide. Either keep it, document it in the tactic tooltip and rules page, and add it to the Rally forecast; or make Rally keep confusion (status priority: confused > inspired).

**Decision (D2):** keep it, and show it in the forecast, the tooltip and the rules page. Planned in T1.4.

### M5. AI never decides about prisoners

**Where:**

- `planAi` in [src/game/campaign.ts:1511](../../src/game/campaign.ts) never issues a `prisoner` command.
- `aiStatecraft` filters out captives (`!o.captive`, line 1369).

**Evidence:** in passive-player probes, 6–22 officers sat captive in AI cities within 2–24 months. Captured player officers stay imprisoned until the player retakes that city.

**Fix:** add an AI prisoner policy per personality.

- Persuade high-value captives using the normal command, with the +20 captive bonus.
- Release low-value captives.
- Execute only for factions whose personality allows it, for example Dong. Record executions in the monthly report when the victim belongs to the player.
- Separately, show the player's own captured officers somewhere. The roster at `App.tsx:1094` hides captives.

**Test:** in a 48-month diagnostics run, the number of AI-held captives stays bounded, and every AI prisoner decision goes through the same validation as the player's.

### M6. Guandu: Ma Teng's second city is undefended

**Where:** [src/content/guandu.ts](../../src/content/guandu.ts). `changan: 'ma'` has no officer in `PLACEMENT`; Ma Teng (stable id `maliang`) is alone in Tianshui.

**Evidence:** turtle probe. Chang'an fell on turn 2 in 3 of 3 seeds. Guandu Liu Bei, played passively, falls on turn 2.

**Fix:**

- Place a second Ma officer in Chang'an, for example a free officer from the roster.
- Review Guandu starting troops. Every officer starts with 700–1,000 troops, so Cao and Yuan can mount joint attacks in the first month.
- Add Guandu to the diagnostics (see H1).

**Test:** a turtle player keeps every starting city for 6 months in at least 2 of 3 seeds, for every Guandu house.

**Decision (D7):** add Ma Chao as a Guandu-only officer in Chang'an, because every one of the 52 roster officers is already placed or deceased in Guandu. Planned in T4.1.

### M7. Early pressure on small houses

**Evidence:**

- A player who does nothing is eliminated in every passive probe, after 2–24 months (190 Liu Bei: month 5; Guandu Liu Bei: month 2).
- A turtle player survives every run.
- Standard difficulty has no opening truce. The AI may attack from turn 1, so a new player has one planning month.

**Fix (design):**

- Give a one-month truce on Standard to houses rated "Challenging", or to every house.
- Make the adviser warn in month 1 when a neighbor can already beat a city (the `weakBorder` advice exists but ranks below food and at-risk).
- Re-check the difficulty ratings with the improved bot.

**Decision (D5):** on Standard, Challenging houses only get an opening truce, plus the month-1 weak-border warning. Planned in T4.2, which also explains why the truce must last two months.

### M8. The opening truce binds only the AI

**Where:**

- `march` in [src/game/campaign.ts:905](../../src/game/campaign.ts) has no truce check.
- `planAi` blocks AI marches only (lines 1275 and 1548).

**Effect:** on Story difficulty the player can attack during the truce, and rivals cannot retaliate with marches.

**Fix:** reject player marches while `state.turn < state.truceUntilTurn`, with a bilingual reason. Alternatively, let a player march end the truce for everyone.

**Test:** march rejected during the truce, atomically.

**Fixed:** `march` rejects every march while `state.turn < state.truceUntilTurn` with "The opening truce forbids attacks until it ends." (translated in `game.zh.ts`). The AI already never marched during the truce, so only the player is affected. Test: `phase3.test.ts` › "the opening truce binds the player too, atomically".

- `getObjectives` only shows the player's own progress.
- The adviser never mentions rivals.

**Fix:**

**Test:** a pure adviser test with a rival at 6 months.

### M10. Auto-resolve can exceed the 50 ms budget

**Evidence:** probe of 30 auto-resolved 10-versus-10 battles (2,000 troops each, mixed types). Median 26.7 ms, worst case 68.5 ms on the development machine. Campaign battles are usually smaller (diagnostics median 3.9 ms, 95th percentile 16.9 ms), but P2-9 sets "< 50 ms per battle on CI".

**Fix:**

- Cache `paths()` per unit per AI turn. `addThreat` and `planUnitV2` recompute Dijkstra for every enemy and every candidate.
- Compute `approachCost` lazily per goal set (already partly done).
- Profile with the 10-versus-10 setup.

**Test:** add a benchmark test that fails above 50 ms for the 95th percentile of 10-versus-10 battles.

- **Purity:**

**What D5 means in practice.**

- Rivals never march in month 1, because `planAi` and `aiJointAttacks` skip turn 0. A one-month truce (`truceUntilTurn = 1`) would therefore protect nobody.
- The plan instead gives Challenging houses the existing two-month truce (`truceUntilTurn = 2`). The first rival march moves from the end of month 2 to the end of month 3, which is one extra month of peace.
- Because of M8, the truce also stops the player marching in months 1 and 2.
- The Challenging houses are Liu Bei in the 190 scenario, and Cao Cao, Liu Bei and Ma Teng in Guandu.

**Why D7 adds an officer.** Every one of the 52 roster officers is already placed or deceased in Guandu, so no free officer could go to Chang'an. Two alternatives were rejected:

- starting Ma Teng in Chang'an, which would leave Tianshui with no officer;
- making Chang'an start neutral, which would leave Ma Teng with one city.

The cost of D7 is that Guandu saves made before the change will no longer load (see T4.1). That is acceptable while v0.2 is unreleased.

What R1.1 found, at 48 months:

- Contested battles (30% of all) already have a median of 5 days, but 52.8% of them end by camp capture.
- Lopsided battles (45%) take 3 days and are 74% move-only.
- Of all move-only AI turns, 45.6% are units held back near their camp and 44.2% have no enemy in reach.
- So for R1.3, the levers that matter most are the hold-near-camp radius, the camp rush in contested fights, and the AI attack margins, more than raw damage.
